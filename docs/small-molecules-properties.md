# Small molecules and properties: individual research and design

Seventeenth lesson, individually reviewed. Research started after the covalent lesson's inspected sample screenshots were sent. Whole-course parity and exam readiness remain open.

## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Let the learner distinguish an attraction inside one molecule from an attraction between two molecules, then predict what survives boiling before changing the particle arrangement. Keep molecule identities and atom totals fixed during phase change. Contrast neutral moving molecules with mobile charged ions, rather than infer conductivity from motion alone.

**Assets?** A paired liquid/gas particle diagram with solid covalent bonds and separately labelled dashed intermolecular attractions. Native prediction controls, a within/between force legend and particle/carrier ledger. A data table supports signed-temperature comparison and an explicitly bounded family-size trend. A 3D asset is optional only if it helps inspect that distinction; another isolated molecule does not demonstrate the bulk property.

**Generic template appropriate?** The navigation and assessment shell can be reused. The preceding electron-sharing controls cannot teach molecular separation or conduction. This workbench must preserve intact bonded groups during a physical change, retain wrong predictions for diagnosis, and separate applied evidence from decorative animation.

## Actual sources read

- AQA Chemistry specification 4.2.2.4, corresponding to Trilogy small-molecule properties: weak intermolecular forces compared with covalent bonds; low melting/boiling points; intermolecular attractions overcome during melting/boiling; size trend; neutral molecules do not carry charge. The specification's adjacent polymer section was read to define the boundary, not counted as taught here.
- Pearson Chemistry specification 1.34 and neighbouring 1.32: typical simple molecular low melting/boiling and poor conduction. Do not generalise all covalent substances as low melting or non-conducting: giant structures and graphite need their own lessons.
- Actual paired AQA June 2023 Higher 04.5 question and scheme: methane versus poly(ethene); causal chain molecule size → intermolecular strength → energy required → state/melting or boiling point. Explicitly disallows weak covalent bonds and breaking covalent bonds. A later polymer lesson must develop repeat-unit and chain representations; this source informs the force distinction here.
- Actual online publisher [OpenStax intermolecular forces m68761](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-chemistry-bundle/blob/db0a8e6027100ce082e67fc8879faab86f9a58a7/modules/m68761/index.cnxml), HTTP 200, 67,783 bytes, saved locally and relevant paragraphs read. HCl phase-change versus bond-breaking energy supports distinct within/between forces. Halogen and unbranched hydrocarbon comparisons support similar-family size trends; shape, polarity and hydrogen bonding limit any unrestricted size-only inference. Those mechanisms are not additional GCSE memorisation requirements in this introduction.

## Planned review risks

Keep a moving gas particle neutral, not an electron or ion. Do not equate boiling with atomisation, broken covalent bonds or chemical reaction. Do not claim all molecular solutions never conduct: acids can form ions in water; distinguish the supplied neutral molecular liquid from an ion-producing aqueous solution. Do not predict melting points solely from atom count across unrelated substances. Molecule positions and gaps are schematic; supplied data, not a decorative particle picture, establish physical values.

## After building: content and student review

**Mistakes corrected?** The first mobile control appeared at 708 pixels, below the original 664-pixel viewport. A shorter title, instruction and visible label corrected this without weakening the assertion or shortening its accessible name. An initial wrong-feedback class called `incorrect` accidentally matched a `/correct/` test and lacked the established retry styling; it now uses the existing retry class. Validation rejects array-valued saved selections instead of coercing them into strings. Final review added an explicit melting task: a boiling-only treatment would leave an actual specification demand underdeveloped.

**Exam-board deviations?** AQA Chemistry 4.2.2.4, Trilogy 5.2.2.4 and Pearson 1.34 were read directly. The lesson develops the common causal requirements. The 2023 Higher marking chain informed original questions, not copied examination content or an official marking-equivalence claim. Polymers, giant structures, detailed acid ionisation and full OCR/whole-course mapping remain separate work. The related-group trend is bounded; advanced polarity and hydrogen bonding are not imposed as memorisation requirements.

**Useful to students?** Students must choose the interaction and predict its consequence before comparing phases. Wrong forces survive the comparison, reload and undo. The intact-molecule inventory makes a physical change distinguishable from atomisation. The carrier work requires both an electrical prediction and a reason: selecting “does not conduct” with an electron-carrier explanation remains wrong. Independent practice combines graphical, numeric, explanatory and property-evidence demands. Written answers remain self-reviewed and stored with false automatic correctness; no short set certifies mastery. These are design judgments, not pupil-trial results.

**Maths UI comparison?** The actual Maths conservation/ratio workbenches and screenshots informed stable quantities, task/answer layout, native operations and specific feedback. For this lesson, the Maths SignedScalingWorkbench was read directly: directed values sit on a scaled number line rather than being compared by magnitude. Chemistry's new boiling-point plot applies that visible signed-position principle to actual supplied data. It retains local fonts, white cards, blue actions and curriculum navigation. A separate 3D molecule was not added: within/between interaction and bulk arrangement are the relevant asset demands here. The final mobile model has the first useful prediction inside the original viewport, and SVG label-size checks require at least 12 rendered pixels. Table, plot and particle diagrams have text equivalents. Long explanation/task sequences still need scrolling; this does not establish app-wide UI parity.
