## Questions to ask before designing
What chemical or practical reasoning changes when the student acts? Are variables actually linked to a specified investigation rather than generic lists? Does the task distinguish initial temperature, reaction-stage extremum and final cooling? Can students read a decimal scale and use matching graph differences rather than divide a temperature by mass? Is a repeat mean valid when initial conditions differ? Are anomalies justified from evidence rather than automatically deleting highest readings? Does insulation reduce transfer rather than abolish it? Does stirring improve representativeness rather than create energy? Does the plan logically sequence measurement and mixing? What can the app honestly establish about practical competence?

## Distinct proposed modes
1. Plan: choose independent variable, dependent measurement, equipment, and genuinely controlled conditions for specified mass/volume investigations. Preserve plausible wrong choices; feedback explains their actual consequence.
2. Observe: select baseline and highest/lowest reaction-stage observation on a calibrated trace; estimate signed change. Original records differ from lesson58; the focus is measurement timing/representativeness, not another exo/endo classification lesson.
3. Repeat: supplied repeated readings, selected retained trials and predicted mean. Include a specified procedural failure, ordinary spread and differing baselines. No automated deletion solely because a value is far from the mean.
4. Graph: construct a gradient triangle by selecting two best-fit coordinates; predict Δtemperature, Δmass, quotient and unit. Also extrapolate the intercept and distinguish fitted estimate from a measured zero-mass observation. Original graphs and numbers, not copied exam images.
5. Evaluate: select claim plus evidence about insulation, stirring, resolution, heat exchange, repetitions, and limits of inference. Method-writing uses rubrics and remains self-reviewed.
