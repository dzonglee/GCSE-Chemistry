# Rates and equilibrium: individual AQA clause evidence

Reviewed 9 October 2026 against the freshly downloaded [AQA 8462 specification](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/chemistry/specifications/AQA-8462-SP-2016.PDF), printed 56–61. The text of all eleven clauses was read; the catalyst profile on page 58 and named reversible examples on page 59 were visually inspected. Full current task prompts, answers/references and relevant native records were examined. This replaces the earlier grouped provisional notes; it changes no lesson questions or interactions.

| Exact clause and tier | Actual teaching and response evidence | Demand and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.6.1.1 Rate calculation; common mean/graphs/tangent construction, Higher mol/s and numerical tangent gradient | `rr-v1-A-interval/A-round/B-consumption/B-graph/B-plateau`, `rr-v1-A-draw`, `rr-v1-p-draw-tangent`, `rr-v1-R-tangent`; Higher `tr-v1-A-gradient/A-moles/A-calibration/A-draw`, `B-consumed/B-minute/B-calibration`, `R-mass` | Independent mean rates use both mass and volume, elapsed-time differences, positive reactant consumption, minutes-to-seconds and retained nonzero plateaus. The common journey constructs observed points and a separate fit; common tangent construction is practice, with delayed explanation. The reserved tangent drawing/numerical gradients are in the Higher journey. Do not relabel a practice drawing as an independent drawing, or supplied axes as arbitrary-scale selection. |
| 4.6.1.2 Rate factors and RP5; common | `rp-v1-p-plan-written/p-endpoint-written/p-dilution-written`; independent `rp-v1-a-plan/a-apparatus/a-plot/a-endpoint/a-dilution`, `b-apparatus/b-plot/b-endpoint/b-dilution`; delayed `ra-evidence/rb-endpoint`; collision and temperature journeys below | Gas-volume and colour/turbidity methods both exist. Full practice plans specify a testable concentration hypothesis, controlled amount/geometry/volume/temperature, gas-tight measurement and timing/repeats, or cross/depth/lighting/observer/acid controls. Independent tasks test confounds, apparatus, records and endpoints. Reciprocal endpoint time is a comparative proxy, not an automatically calibrated g/s or instantaneous rate. Physical apparatus competence requires supervised work. |
| 4.6.1.3 Collision theory and activation; common | `ct-v1-check-a-energy/check-b-energy/check-a-compress/check-a-area/check-a-explain/check-b-explain`, `ct-v1-review-a-explain/review-b-explain`; `tc-v1-a1/b4/b5` | Independent energy thresholds, number/volume ratios, fixed-temperature compression, separated/wetted cube areas, powder-versus-chip explanations and delayed inaccessible-interface reasoning are present. Heating changes speed/collision frequency and the sufficiently energetic fraction. Simple proportional comparisons use supplied counts, areas and measured rates; density doubling does not establish an exact doubled chemical rate without a stated relation or data. |
| 4.6.1.4 Catalysts; common | `tc-v1-p-not-inert/p-enzyme/p-enzyme-limits`, `tc-v1-a3/a5/b1/b3`, `tc-v1-ra2/ra3/rb2/rb3`; the Haber journey's iron-catalyst conditions | The overall hydrogen-peroxide equation explicitly places the catalyst above the arrow and distinguishes regeneration from being inert. Independent/delayed responses explain lower activation, unchanged kinetic energy at fixed temperature, profile endpoints and insufficient controlled evidence. Enzymes are explicitly biological catalysts with suitable substrates/conditions. AQA does not require arbitrary additional catalyst names beyond those specified elsewhere; the named Haber catalyst is taught there. |
| 4.6.2.1 Reversibility; common | `re-v1-g-direction`, `re-v1-p-hydrate`; actual `directionRecords.initial/hydrate/hydrateReverse` | The reversible arrow, heated ammonium chloride and recombination on cooling, and blue hydrated copper sulfate/white anhydrous salt plus water are taught. Changing conditions can reverse the direction; the qualitative teaching record is not certification of handling these chemicals. |
| 4.6.2.2 Equal-and-opposite energy; common | `re-v1-g-energy`, independent `re-v1-a-energy/b-batch`, delayed `re-v1-ra-energy/rb-water` | A forward +30 kJ batch reverses to −30 kJ; an independent −36 kJ batch reverses to +36 kJ. Batch scaling uses the same dry-salt basis. Energy transfer is not the activation barrier, and equal magnitude applies to the same reaction amount. |
| 4.6.2.3 Closed dynamic equilibrium; common | `re-v1-a-written/b-written`, delayed `re-v1-ra-written/rb-amounts`; turnover/rates/boundary/evidence records | Full independent responses explain continuing equal positive rates, closed matter boundaries and unequal constant inventories, and reject open-flow steady inventories as proof of chemical equilibrium. Zero net change does not mean either direction has stopped. |
| 4.6.2.4 Le Chatelier and qualitative changes; Higher | `es-v1-a-conflict`, `es-v1-b-equal`, delayed `es-v1-ra-partial` | Independent reasoning uses supplied reaction direction/energy and gaseous coefficients. Opposing pressure/cooling changes cannot establish a net yield change without their relative effects. Counteraction can be partial rather than exactly restoring the old composition. |
| 4.6.2.5 Concentration changes; Higher | `es-v1-a-add/b-removal`, delayed `es-v1-ra-partial` | Supplied before/immediate/later inventories distinguish added reactant, removed product, conserved total after the disturbance and forward replenishment. A later 1.6 mmol after removal from 2 to 1 mmol is partial replenishment, not exact reversal of removal. |
| 4.6.2.6 Temperature changes; Higher | `es-v1-a-temperature/b-temperature`, delayed `es-v1-rb-temperature`; competing-change explanation above | Independent examples heat an endothermic forward reaction and cool an exothermic forward reaction; delayed cooling of an endothermic forward reaction favours the exothermic reverse. Reaction rate and relative equilibrium amount are separate demands. |
| 4.6.2.7 Gas pressure; Higher | `es-v1-a-phase/b-coefficients/a-conflict/b-equal`, delayed `es-v1-ra-pressure` | Independent/delayed responses count only gaseous coefficients, distinguish smaller/larger sides, and explain no position shift for equal gas totals in 2HI ⇌ H2 + I2. Pressure changes are stated through volume at fixed temperature; solid coefficients do not become gas coefficients. |
