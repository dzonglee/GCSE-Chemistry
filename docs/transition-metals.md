# Transition metals — individual source, design and UI review

## Compare with GCSE Maths before building

**Is this material at the Maths app's standard?** The preliminary route offered a generic prediction model and a few recall items. Maths's actual task workbenches preserve a student's operation, diagnose an error, and connect that operation to a specific learning demand. This lesson needs precise physical comparisons, charge accounting and interpretation of catalyst evidence, rather than another general periodic-table sandbox. The new task-specific models address that local gap; they do not establish parity for the remaining course.

**How can this lesson be interesting and interactive?** Let students select two proposed comparison statements, including true statements that fail a physical-property request. Require distinct properties and directions. Keep iron's 26 protons fixed while the electron count changes from neutral to Fe²⁺ and from Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺. Ask students to predict early and completed product amounts separately using supplied catalyst readings.

**What assets can I build?** An original accessible reference table for sodium and six specified transition-metal examples; a fixed-positive/variable-negative charge ledger; a two-series SVG plot and equivalent data table. No invented full iron shell arrangement or unnecessary 3D molecular asset. The existing page layout and typography are reused because they improve navigation and consistency; the scientific manipulation is new.

**What differs from other lessons?** This is not Group 1's single-electron loss, Group 7's incoming-electron/displacement reasoning, or Group 0's stable full shell. Multiple iron charges preserve the element identity. Physical-versus-chemical comparison is itself an assessed demand. Catalysis concerns reaction rate, not creation of extra product atoms.

**Generic template or different lesson?** Reuse the warm-up/guided/practice/check/review navigation and saved-evidence rules. Use distinct property-selection, charge and graph models inside it. The content order introduces the physical comparison first, then variable charges, then catalytic evidence; practice fades the models and adds evidence limits and explanation.

## Actual materials read

- [AQA Chemistry 4.1.3.1–4.1.3.2](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/chemistry/specifications/AQA-8462-SP-2016.PDF): separate Chemistry only. Physical comparisons include melting points, densities, strength and hardness; chemical comparisons include oxygen, water and halogens. Named examples Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu. Many form ions with different charges, coloured compounds and catalysts.
- [Pearson Chemistry, March 2024, 5.1C](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/Science/2016/Specification/gcse-chemistry-spec.pdf): separate Chemistry, typical high melting point/density, coloured compounds and catalytic activity exemplified by iron. Corrosion and alloys are following requirements, not implicitly completed here.
- [AQA June 2022 Foundation 09.2–09.3 paper](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2022/june/AQA-84621F-QP-JUN22.PDF) and [actual paired scheme](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2022/june/AQA-84621F-MS-JUN22.PDF): unfamiliar R²⁺/R³⁺ used as classification evidence; physical-comparison scheme accepts lower Group 1 melting/boiling points/density, strength and hardness, and ignores coloured compounds/different charges/chemical references for that physical request. App questions and diagrams are original.
- [AQA June 2023 Foundation 06.3 paper](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-84621F-QP-JUN23.PDF) and [paired scheme](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-84621F-MS-JUN23.PDF): copper is harder and less reactive than sodium. The question's general request permits these two types of property; the app distinguishes this from an explicitly physical-only request.
- [AQA June 2019 Foundation 02.3 paper](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2019/june/AQA-84621F-QP-JUN19.PDF) and [correct W-MS paired scheme](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2019/june/AQA-84621F-W-MS-JUN19.PDF): transition metals often act as catalysts. The original app transfers to interpreting data rather than copying a question bank.
- AQA 4.6.1.4: catalysts provide an alternative lower-activation-energy pathway and are not used up overall; different reactions require different catalysts. AQA 4.10.4.1: iron catalyst in the Haber process. Full kinetics and equilibrium are deferred.
- AQA 4.8.3.2: supplied coloured-hydroxide examples Cu(II) blue, Fe(II) green, Fe(III) brown. These are compound examples, without teaching the later analytical procedure or claiming full ion-test coverage.
- [OpenStax transition-metals module](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-chemistry-bundle/blob/db0a8e6027100ce082e67fc8879faab86f9a58a7/modules/m68842/index.cnxml): actual paragraphs read on high-melting/hard metals, cations, non-metal reactions often needing heat, common Fe(II)/Fe(III) and broader variable oxidation states. The strict d-orbital definition is beyond this GCSE lesson; group-12 categorisation is not used to teach mercury as an unquestioned transition-metal exception. Advanced orbital arrangements are excluded.
- Publisher m68843 paragraphs on coloured coordination compounds and different colours depending on the actual compound; m68844 colour theory reviewed as beyond scope. The app does not claim that all compounds of one metal have one colour.
- [Secondary reference data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Bowserinator/Periodic-Table-JSON/master/PeriodicTableJSON.json): actually read Na and the six selected metals' melting temperatures/densities. Converted K to °C and rounded melting values; supplied density units g cm⁻³. This is secondary reference teaching data, not official exam-board measurement data. The catalyst readings are explicitly original illustrative data, not a real experimental result or fitted reaction law.

Each answer, value, hint and misconception was reviewed. Iron's positive charge increases with electron loss; proton number and identity do not change. Roman numerals denote the simple iron ion's charge, not atom count. FeCl₃ uses one 3+ ion balanced by three 1− ions. No first-20 shell filling rule is applied to iron. Classification evidence is phrased as most consistent, not proof of a unique element. Less reactive does not imply inability to form oxides/halides. General comparisons are not asserted as universal laws.

The catalyst model compares identical initial reactants and temperature, and supplied completed runs with equal final amounts. This is not a universal claim that every equilibrium yield is independent of conditions. Blue solid and brown dashed curves have explicit text and an equivalent table. The plot joins seven original readings and does not manufacture a numerical kinetic law.

## After building: mistakes, scope and student usefulness

**Did I make mistakes?** The first render review found that iron(III) feedback could blur total loss from neutral with the one additional loss from Fe²⁺. Corrected both feedback and ledger wording. Mobile catalyst axis labels were too small; increased their actual rendered sizes. The legacy mercury example was removed because broad d-block inclusion is not the strict transition-element definition. No existing saved question identity was deleted.

**Does anything differ from board requirements?** The route correctly remains Foundation/separate Chemistry. AQA requires a broader set of comparisons/examples than Pearson's 5.1C, so it follows that broader working baseline. The hydroxide examples and catalyst pathway anticipate later topics but do not claim to complete ion analysis, rates, equilibrium, corrosion or alloys. Full qualification mapping and OCR review remain open.

**Would I find this useful as a student?** It gives an operation to connect charge arithmetic to different iron ions, concrete data for comparisons, and feedback on true-but-irrelevant answers. Faded practice and reserved checks test recall and transfer without the model. The independent written response explains what a mark-scheme-oriented comparison needs. This is an evidence-based design judgment, not a report of actual pupil testing; explanations remain self-reviewed.

**How does the UI differ from Maths, and what improved?** It shares Maths's white cards, cool-grey page, blue actions, task-first layout and visible journey. The scientific models now preserve wrong selections and support native keyboard/touch controls, undo and reload. Chemistry needs readable units, superscript charges, graph/table alternatives and compound-versus-element distinctions. The mobile graph label correction addresses a genuine legibility gap. Final checks passed 68 unit and 186 full desktop/mobile browser cases, followed by ten final lesson-specific checks including actual graph-label rendering size of at least 12 pixels. Types, lint, formatting and build passed. Final samples were inspected and sent before ionic-bonding work; no whole-course parity is claimed.
