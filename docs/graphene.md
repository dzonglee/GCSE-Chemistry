# Graphene: individual research and design

Twenty-first lesson, individually reviewed. Research began after the graphite screenshots and actual GLB were inspected and sent. Fullerenes/nanotubes and polymers require their own later lessons.

## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Inspect one extended hexagonal sheet edge-on and face-on, then distinguish atom-layer count from bonded-neighbour count. Match a thin conducting layer to electronics using mobile electrons, and evaluate supplied composite-panel evidence against simultaneous mass/load requirements. Do not treat graphene as three stacked graphite sheets or as a finite C₃₂ molecule.

**Assets?** A real rotatable/exportable single-sheet geometry, accessible projection and independent cropped drawing. Carbon centres lie in one plane; enlarged spheres do not show physical thickness. Strong in-plane bonds and delocalised electrons support different property explanations. An original clearly illustrative comparison table should show whole composite-panel masses and supported loads, not invented physical measurements of an atomic sheet. Data support a particular candidate; they do not prove every graphene composite is stronger or commercially ready.

**Generic template?** Reuse task navigation and the validated honeycomb topology, but a three-layer sliding interaction teaches graphite, not graphene. The lesson needs single-sheet inspection and property/use decisions with causal explanations and multi-criterion evidence. A novel-material name or decorative picture is insufficient.

## Actual sources read

- AQA Chemistry 4.2.3.3 and Trilogy 5.2.3.3 actual text: graphene is a single layer of graphite, properties suitable for electronics/composites, explain structure/bonding and recognise diagrams. Fullerenes are separate hollow molecular shapes; their cage/tube demands will receive their own lesson.
- Pearson 1.38 actual text: explain graphene/fullerenes properties using structure and bonding.
- Paired June 2022 Higher 03.3 actual paper and scheme: a C₇₀ hollow molecule is fullerene, with graphene as a distractor. This is a recognition contrast, not a graphene strength measurement. No unretrieved graphene-specific mark scheme is claimed.
- OpenStax m68770 actual graphene paragraph and publisher figure fetched HTTP 200 and inspected. The sheet is one atom thick, strong/lightweight and electrically conducting; electronics and structural applications are potential uses. The original schematic compares sheet, cage, tube and stacked layers. Its “formed into” caption must not be interpreted as simply folding an unchanged all-hexagon sheet into a closed fullerene: cage topology is separate later work.

## Review points to resolve

Verify one planar connected honeycomb sheet, three neighbours per selected interior carbon, actual GLB coordinates and no interlayer bonds. Distinguish strength from toughness, sample mass from density and a possible use from guaranteed suitability. Preserve wrong layer/count/causal predictions, support touch/key controls, deferred checks and delayed retrieval. ## Geometry and evidence review

The actual GLB has 32 carbon meshes and 40 covalent edges, one plane of carbon centres and a connected honeycomb graph. Selected sites have three equal-length coplanar vectors at 120°. These are schematic coordinates and enlarged atoms, not measured layer thickness. There are no interlayer links. Network-link metadata does not imply isolated localised single bonds in a delocalised structure.

Equivalent layer facts share exposure across teaching, practice, checks and delayed review. The guided asset also exposes the three-neighbour fact, so the equivalent graphene/graphite local-coordination questions share aliases. Starting a new form or course session does not make exposed answers fresh. Whole-set feedback is deferred; drafts survive reload; delayed review requires seven elapsed days.

## Student usefulness and limits

The lesson supports diagram recognition, distinct property explanations, relevant use reasoning, quantitative evidence transfer and bounded inference. It does not guarantee device/composite suitability, equal physical dimensions, actual fracture performance or universal behaviour of all covalent materials. Selected AQA/Trilogy/Pearson reading is narrower than full-board certification. Fullerenes, nanotubes, remaining preliminary lessons and whole-course exam readiness remain open.
