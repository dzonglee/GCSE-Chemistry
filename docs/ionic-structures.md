## Before building: the user's design questions

**Is this interesting and interactive?** The specific challenge is to see beyond the front layer of a drawing and explain how charged particles can still fail to conduct. Learners select an interior sodium or chloride ion, rotate a genuine 3D lattice fragment and propose its opposite nearest-neighbour count. Phase-specific controls require both a conductivity prediction and its carrier explanation, preserving plausible wrong electron-based answers.

**What assets does this lesson need?** An actual rotatable/exportable 64-ion NaCl fragment, an equivalent oblique projection, a finite labelled 2D slice for independent formula interpretation, and separate solid/molten/aqueous particle representations. Molten or dissolved ions must not be represented by an unchanged rigid solid lattice. No remote image or video is needed.

**What distinct demands make a generic template insufficient?** Ionic formation is not the same learning demand as lattice properties. The structure is extended, forces act in all directions, empirical formulas describe ratios, and conductivity needs charge mobility as well as charge. The exact six-neighbour NaCl geometry must not become a rule for all ionic structures. A generic material selector or decoration cannot establish these explanations.

## Scientific and state review

The finite 4×4×4 checkerboard of ion centres contains 32 Na⁺ and 32 Cl⁻, net charge zero. Alternating species form the rock-salt arrangement. Selected interior ions have six nearest opposite ions along ±x, ±y and ±z. Edge ions have fewer neighbours displayed because the rest of the lattice is omitted; the UI explicitly labels this fragment. It is not a crystallographic unit cell, isolated molecule or universal coordination-number model. Highlight colours are inspection aids, not species changes. Rotation is genuine 3D, event-driven, with keyboard/buttons/touch and an oblique 2D/text fallback. Export must be verified as actual GLB geometry.

Phase drawings each show four ions of each charge, preserving net charge zero while changing order/mobility. Solid positions alternate in both axes; molten/solution diagrams are disordered. Opposite arrow directions represent ion movement under an applied field, without a measured-current or unsupervised heating claim. Water molecules are explicitly omitted from the solution diagram. Ionic conductivity is not moving electrons. High melting/boiling explanations name strong electrostatic attractions and substantial energy; they do not invent weak forces between NaCl molecules or covalent shared pairs.

Native prediction controls precede the asset. Strict model states preserve wrong guesses; histories permit one choice operation per step and bounded saved history. Independent checks omit the learning workbench and defer feedback until whole-set submission. Written explanations remain self-reviewed and never automatically correct. Legacy data and original question identities are preserved.

## After building: required review questions

**Did I make mistakes?** Authoring review corrected the simplified solid diagram's second row to alternate both horizontally and vertically, and added a no-change/history-limit guard to native selections. Type-checking caught missing journey metadata and a missing browser stub `this` annotation before release. Native selectors received explicit accessible names after the exact-label browser check exposed option-text interference. The GLB verifier now handles valid glTF matrix transforms as well as translation vectors and checks the exact four depth layers. Control-label margins were reduced after visual comparison.

**Does anything differ from exam-board requirements?** This targets the common AQA/Pearson giant ionic structure and property demands, including model limitations and empirical ratios. It does not claim Pearson's complete cross-material comparisons, universal solubility, detailed structures of every ionic compound, electrolysis chemistry or complete OCR mapping. The six-neighbour count is a teaching aid for familiar NaCl, bounded rather than an invented all-compound requirement.

**Would this be useful to a student?** The model makes a missing-depth error inspectable; the paired phase predictions expose the tempting but invalid “charged therefore conducts” explanation. Independent practice requires interpreting a different representation and constructing the causal chain that selected schemes demand. This is design judgment, not pupil-study evidence. Written self-review remains a limitation to audit across the course.

**How does the UI compare with Maths, and what should improve?** It follows the actual rendered Maths task-first layout, native controls and conserved quantities rather than merely adding navigation. Chemistry needs a genuine 3D representation here and a clear text equivalent; phase reasoning deserves its own model. The longer mobile asset/card layout requires actual viewport, font-size, reflow and screenshot inspection before the next lesson. Final desktop/mobile lattice and dissolved-ion screenshots were inspected: the first useful native control is within the original tested viewport, diagrams retain readable labels, and the native fields now have compact consistent spacing.
