## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Predict dissolved-solute mass per final solution volume. Construct cm³→dm³ working before using a concentration equation; rearrange for solute mass/solution volume; distinguish an aliquot from dilution of the whole solution. Compare separately supplied sample amounts and volumes. Retain wrong unit denominators, reversed ratios and confusion with solvent/whole-solution mass.

**Assets?** A clearly labelled unit-volume ledger and two quantity axes; an actual rotating/exportable 3D volume accounting asset when useful, with fixed identified macroscopic solute markers. Each marker represents a specified mass of dissolved solute, not a single molecule or ion. Solvent particles are omitted and container dimensions are schematic. A change in final volume must preserve all solute-marker identities in a dilution comparison; the scene must not imply that every solution is coloured or that all substances can dissolve at all chosen concentrations.

**Specific demands?** Concentration uses dissolved solute mass divided by final **solution** volume. It is not solution density, not mass divided by starting solvent volume and not a pure-compound element percentage. g/dm³, g/cm³ and g/L must be distinguished and converted explicitly. A sample of a homogeneous solution has the same concentration but proportionally less dissolved mass. Diluting an entire retained sample changes concentration without losing its solute; aliquot removal is a different action.

**Tier choice?** Core g/dm³ calculation and rearrangement are common scope. Current AQA 4.3.2.5/Trilogy5.3.2.5 explicitly label explanations of how mass/volume relate to concentration as **Higher only**. The core lesson will use four different calculation demands: unit rate, quantity selection, solute mass and solution volume. Conceptual proportional/dilution explanations will receive a separate Higher lesson, individually reviewed after this sample gate. Pearson1.49 explicitly includes g/dm³ concentration calculations. Separate mol/dm³ and titration calculations remain later lessons. No bulk change to global task-tier infrastructure is required to label a clearly bounded extension.
