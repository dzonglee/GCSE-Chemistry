# Ionic bonding — individual source, design and UI review

## Before building: Maths comparison and lesson-specific decisions

**Is my material at the GCSE Maths standard?** The preliminary route mixed electron-loss recall, formula ratios and lattice statements behind a generic classification model. Maths's actual FractionRegroupWorkbench preserves each exchange, represents both parts of a conserved total and explains the state rather than merely navigate a lesson. Ionic formation needs a corresponding transfer operation with separate donor/recipient counts, diagram origins and error repair. The new journey addresses that local deficit; the whole app remains incomplete.

**How can this be interesting and interactive?** Transfer one electron at a time. Keep every nucleus fixed and maintain the whole-particle electron count. Let magnesium's two electrons go to the wrong chlorine recipient, preserving overall charge/electrons while failing to form two Cl⁻ ions. Ask students to repair the distribution. Compare one Mg donating two electrons to O with two Na atoms each donating one.

**What assets can I build?** Original dot-and-cross outer-shell diagrams with brackets and displayed charges, charged-particle readouts, a conservation ledger and four controlled transfer examples. Electron origins need a readable 2D diagram aligned with exam representation; a giant-lattice 3D asset belongs to the following structural lesson. No decorative bond connector should turn transfer into a covalent pair.

**What differs from other lessons?** Earlier ion lessons change one particle's electron count. Here the same electrons move between particles and the individual recipient distribution matters. The ionic bond is attraction between the resulting opposite charges, not merely the transfer event. Formula subscripts describe a smallest ratio, not isolated molecules. Dot/cross totals, origins, brackets and charges each carry information.

**Generic template or different lesson?** Reuse the journey stages, task-card layout, native controls, saved history and conservative assessment rules. Replace the broad classification sandbox with a matrix of permitted donor/recipient operations and current ion diagrams. Four guided examples cover Group 1/2 metals with Group 6/7 non-metals: NaCl, MgCl₂, MgO and Na₂O.

Every value, answer, hint and misconception was checked. Na/Cl atomic numbers 11/17; Mg/O 12/8. Correct transfers conserve 28 electrons in NaCl, 46 across Mg and two Cl particles, 20 in MgO and 30 across two Na particles and one O. Correct donor electron counts are ten in these examples; chloride has 18 and oxide ten. Protons are unchanged; total shown-system charge remains zero even before the intended ions are fully formed.

A wrong MgCl₂ distribution of two to one Cl gives a displayed outer count nine for that recipient and seven for the other, not two stable chloride ions. This wrong state is retained for diagnosis, never accepted as intended formation. The model limits each donor's loss to the available initial outer electrons and allows undo/return. Histories require a single electron operation; no two-electron jump is silently inferred.

The actual AQA PDF page 27 diagram was rendered and inspected: its fully formed metal ion omits electron shells, while the non-metal preserves the transferred marker and both electron arrangements are written separately. The official example uses sodium dots and chlorine crosses; the app uses the opposite marker styling with an explicit legend, preserving the same one-transferred/seven-original counts. Marker styles are interchangeable, but their origins must be consistent. The app follows that transfer-diagram convention: fully formed Na⁺/Mg²⁺ omit their shell markers and show full counts/arrangements separately. After complete outer-electron loss, the previous inner shell is now the outer occupied shell, but the simplified transfer symbol does not redraw those former inner electrons as new markers. Whole-particle conservation explicitly includes inner electrons omitted from the outer-only diagrams. Dots/crosses track origin and all electrons remain the same kind of particle.

Ion brackets and charge signs are shown for the current model particle. Independent proposed diagrams deliberately keep a wrong total or wrong origin/charge representation after an answer, so the original error remains available for inspection. Given Al³⁺ is a formula-ratio extension, not a claim to predict Group 3 formation under the specified Group 1/2 rule. Na⁺ is not neon merely because both are 2,8. Ratios do not describe molecules, and conductivity is not claimed complete here.

## After building: required review questions

**Did I make mistakes?** Review of the actual official diagram corrected a representation mismatch: completed cation shells are omitted in the transfer drawing rather than displaying eight former-inner-shell crosses as newly visible transfer markers. Review also made the previous-inner-shell explanation and inclusion of omitted electrons in the conservation ledger explicit, and distinguished current chloride/oxide ion names from neutral chlorine/oxygen atoms. The deliberately wrong cold diagrams are labelled proposed rather than presented as correct reference diagrams. Exact counts use zero numeric tolerance; donor-budget validation and strict histories reject non-integer/tampered transfers. The first full regression exposed an obsolete sandbox test bound to the now-rebuilt route; its full assertions were preserved on the remaining preliminary covalent route. Independent diagram construction was added after the explicit draw requirement review, and its charge selector received a stable accessible name. A dedicated test preserves wrong origin totals, a wrong charge and missing brackets before correction and reload. Final counts and screenshots are recorded after inspection.

**Does anything differ from board requirements?** This is common Foundation/combined content under the stated AQA/Pearson scope. Charge prediction and transfer construction stay within Groups 1/2 and 6/7, using first-20 arrangements. Lattice structure, conductivity and broader naming/polyatomic formulas are separated into following lessons. This review does not claim completion of all Pearson 1.21–1.27 or complete OCR mapping.

**Would I find this useful as a student?** The operations make electron loss and gain a single conserved transfer, diagnose a plausible distribution error, and connect origin symbols to the exam diagram. Four cases make the ion-ratio reasoning visible rather than require formula memorisation alone. Independent diagrams test whether the student can inspect errors without the model, and independent construction requires choosing dots, crosses, charge and brackets with the learner’s own proposed diagram retained. The constructed explanation requires the linked marking demands. This is a design judgment, not actual pupil-study evidence; written marking remains self-reviewed.

**How does the UI differ from Maths, and what improved?** It uses the same task-first layout and clear actions, with Chemistry-specific charged diagrams and per-particle readouts. Native 44-pixel transfer controls, text equivalents, undo/reload and stacked mobile cards are intended to support the same interaction standard. Actual final desktop/mobile views and checks will establish the bounded local result; the whole-app comparison remains open.
