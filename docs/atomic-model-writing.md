# Atomic models: constructed explanations and readable diagrams

Accepted individual correction, 7 October 2026. The original 37 activities already provide useful charge-distribution/approach controls, historical diagrams, explanatory feedback and two written practices. The narrower gap was independently constructing the explanation after learning, rather than selecting a supplied account.

## Decisions for this lesson

**Maths comparison.** Actual `explaining-scatter-conclusions` source was read, including its guided prediction and written evidence-limit tasks. Four freshly loaded desktop/mobile guided/written views were personally inspected. Chemistry retains its own scientific demand: separate observations from inferred charge, size and mass, and explain why contradictory evidence changed a historical model. Shared white cards, blue actions and journey navigation support that demand; a common shell alone is not a learning interaction.

**Interest and interaction.** The existing three scattering tasks let students change distributed positive material to a central positive region, select far/near/head-on approaches and compare changing predictions with fixed gold-foil observations. Native keyboard/touch, wrong answers, undo, reset and reload remain. A new recovery distinguishes electrostatic repulsion from a solid-wall collision. A new written practice combines common and rare observations with the reason for revision.

**Assets and presentation.** Original SVG diagrams are suitable for qualitative charge/path evidence. The workbench does not calculate forces, angles or probabilities. Real 3D counting assets remain in the relevant particle lessons. Tiny word labels were replaced with readable text legends; schematic charge glyphs were enlarged. Supplied historical diagrams have a labelled preview and a keyboard-accessible enlarged view with readable charge symbols. They remain supplied information, not a hidden answer to the new independent writing tasks. The nuclear centre and particles are explicitly magnified, not to scale.

**Different task demands.** Describing plum pudding, comparing charge/electron arrangements, explaining common/rare scattering, explaining electron discovery and correcting a false centre claim are separate demands. Detailed Bohr/Chadwick experimental procedures remain outside this AQA scope. Existing later refinements and percentage interpretation are retained; original fictional datasets are still labelled illustrative, not historical measurements.

**Student usefulness.** Written construction can expose a misconception hidden by recognising a supplied account. Criteria distinguish positive-positive repulsion from attraction, tiny concentrated structure from a large centre, and the discovery of electrons from later nuclear evidence. This is a design judgment supported by selected materials and actual checks, not a report of measured pupil outcomes or whole-course readiness.
