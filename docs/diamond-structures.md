# Diamond and giant covalent structures: individual research and design

Nineteenth lesson, individually reviewed. Research started after the metallic lesson's inspected desktop/mobile gate. The existing combined carbon route will be refocused on diamond and giant-network reasoning; graphite and graphene/fullerenes require their own later individual lessons. Legacy identities remain retained.

## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Rotate an actual three-dimensional covalent network, inspect four neighbours of an interior carbon and distinguish that connected network from four-atom or five-atom molecules. Predict the interaction and energy demand involved in changing the bulk structure; compare this with intact small-molecule boiling from lesson seventeen.

**Assets?** A genuine 3D diamond-network fragment with selected interior atoms and four bond directions, an accessible projection fallback, actual GLB export and text counts. Selected motifs remain connected to neighbouring motifs. Boundary atoms have omitted continuation bonds: a finite drawing is not evidence of lower bulk valency. Native controls should permit wrong coordination, force and carrier predictions before feedback. A silica diagram/property example recognises a compound network without claiming every covalent network is pure carbon or that every covalent substance fails to conduct.

**Generic template?** Use the established navigation/response shell. Neither a finished methane molecule nor the old ionic-conductivity slider can teach an extended directional carbon network. Reuse proven 3D controls/export infrastructure, but implement and independently verify the diamond connectivity itself. Hardness must mean resistance to scratching/cutting, not an assertion that diamond can never fracture.

## Actual sources read

- AQA Chemistry 4.2.2.6 and 4.2.3.1, and actual Trilogy 5.2.2.6/5.2.3.1 text: giant networks, diamond/graphite/silica examples, strong covalent bonds throughout, very high melting, four bonds per diamond carbon, hardness and lack of conduction. Graphite's contrasting carriers/layers are explicitly separate next work. The adjacent small-molecule explanation must not be applied to this network.
- Pearson 1.35–1.37: diamond/graphite are forms of carbon and giant covalent; structure reasoning; diamond in cutting tools. Pearson representation limits were read in the previous metallic review and apply here too.
- Actual paired AQA June 2022 Higher 03.1–03.2: structure description separately credits giant structure, covalent bonding and four bonds per carbon. Melting explanation requires strong bonds, many bonds broken and much energy. A short “strong forces” answer alone does not develop the full assessed chain.
- Actual paired June 2022 Foundation 06.3–06.4: four bonds and very hard property. These demands inform original questions and independent transfer; the app does not reproduce the actual exam questions as reserved forms.
- Online publisher OpenStax m68770, HTTP 200, actual giant-network paragraphs and original network figure inspected. Diamond local four-neighbour motifs connect into the larger network; silica shows a compound network; the caption explicitly distinguishes graphite's noncovalent between-layer interactions. The graphite illustration's uniform-looking interlayer rods must not be copied as strong covalent interlayer bonds. Broad textbook melting numbers are not treated as supplied GCSE measurements or a room-pressure experimental instruction.

Highlight colour/thickness identifies a site; it does not mean a different bond strength.

## Mistakes, boundaries and student usefulness

The first mobile browser pass found a useful control below the original 664-pixel viewport. Short task text reduced the gap, but the long lesson title still put it below the screen; the lesson heading became “Diamond and covalent networks”. The scientific scope still explicitly teaches giant covalent structures. No viewport or touch-height assertion was relaxed. The fallback test initially matched both feedback and the WebGL-status announcement; its selector now checks the actual feedback element while retaining the separate fallback assertion.

The old generic bond sandbox became unreachable when this last preliminary bonding route received its individual journey. Its conductivity regression was replaced with actual reviewed ionic solid/molten wrong-prediction and reload checks; graphite has not been tested or released as an individual lesson. It is next separate work, alongside later graphene/fullerenes and polymers.

Selected AQA/Trilogy/Pearson requirements and paired 2022 papers inform the design. This does not certify every board, every covalent material, real diamond surface chemistry, doped diamond behaviour or the whole course. The lesson supports local/network recognition, complete property explanations, use reasoning and misconception repair; full-course exam readiness remains unverified.
