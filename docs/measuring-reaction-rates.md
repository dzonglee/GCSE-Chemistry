## Questions before authoring

What makes this lesson interesting? A curve can fall while the reaction keeps producing material, a plateau can contain a nonzero amount, and a balance can fall although no atoms disappear. The same dataset yields different mean rates over different selected intervals.

What meaningful interactions should it contain? Independently enter both quantity and time differences and the rate; convert mixed minutes/seconds; interpret raw balance readings; place each observation and draw a separate best-fit curve without modifying the anomaly; interpret rising, falling and plateau curves; compare rate and final yield separately; choose measurement methods and justify controls/calibration.

Does a generic reaction-speed widget suffice? No. It neither teaches raw endpoint subtraction nor graph construction or the limits of a sensor signal. Use dedicated interval/mass/plot/trend/compare/evidence modes. Retain wrong scientific predictions, keyboard/touch and raw invalid inputs.

Would3D help? Yes for an open mass-loss flask on a balance: actual open neck, porous cotton-wool representation, liquid/chips, escaping-gas boundary and balance make loss versus destruction clear. Explicit macroscopic schematic; no fake measured particles, speed or universal mass derived from geometry. A gas-syringe alternative can remain a labelled diagram if no interactive geometry supports a useful task.3D does not improve the numerical graph itself.
