# Group 0: noble gases — individual review

## Questions before authoring

**What does Maths teach through its interactions?** Read the adjacent FactorListWorkbench and LearningOverview: a student proposes an operation, the visible state preserves it, feedback explains the result, and saved evidence supports a next step. Apply that to a gas choice AND its justification rather than display a generic periodic-table widget. Use the existing shell workbench only where shell construction is the actual chemical task.

**What is this lesson's specific reasoning?** A full first shell contains two electrons: helium is not deficient. Argon's stable 2,8,8 arrangement explains why its atoms do not easily transfer or share electrons. Individual noble-gas atoms contrast with halogens' bonded pairs. Boiling-point trends require signed-number reasoning. Density, non-flammability and chemical inertness explain different requirements and cannot substitute for one another.

**Which assets serve those demands?** Deliberate electron placement with an optional actual rotatable 3D helium atom; a supplied gas-property table; original balloon/filament requirements diagrams; independent signed-temperature prediction. Do not add molecule bonds to monatomic noble gases. The atom's nucleus remains magnified and shell positions schematic. The helium scene represents a selected isotope, not an average atomic mass.

**What will wrong answers reveal?** Two electrons placed in the wrong shell; Group 0 misunderstood as no electrons; eight outer confused with eight total; hydrogen selected only because it is light; argon selected only because it does not burn; negative boiling points reversed; electrical glow treated as proof of a new compound. Keep the student's proposal until they repair it.

Every statement, value, answer, hint and misconception response was checked against these bounded source requirements. The first-20 shell rule is not extended to krypton or xenon; these appear only in the supplied physical trend. Helium's unusual lack of normal-pressure freezing is not modelled, and no below-boiling temperature is falsely declared liquid without a melting bound. The room-state question supplies temperatures and pressure context. Boiling concerns interatomic attractions; it does not strip electrons or break invented Ne₂ bonds. No chemical-reactivity trend is inferred from the physical trend.

The filament task specifies argon, while feedback acknowledges that other suitable inert gases can work. It does not teach argon as uniquely inert. The balloon task requires both lift and non-flammability; correct gas with incomplete justification is diagnosed separately. Requirements diagrams are explanations, not apparatus instructions.
