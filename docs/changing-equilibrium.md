# Changing equilibrium — individual lesson review

Lesson 70, Higher tier. The preceding lesson teaches what equilibrium is; this lesson asks what happens immediately after a specified change and how the reacting system subsequently responds. Whole-course parity and exam readiness remain unfinished.

## Questions before building

**Is it interesting and interactive?** Learners distinguish immediate physical change from subsequent chemical response, construct gas-coefficient totals, predict energy and concentration responses, and decide when opposing effects provide insufficient information.

**Which assets does it need?** An actual rotatable, keyboard-operable, downloadable 3D gas container for compression; equation/phase tally for pressure; energy endpoints for temperature; concentration tables; separate pressure/temperature predictions; and sampled evidence plots. One generic slider would obscure these different mechanisms.

**What does this lesson uniquely need?** Fixed-temperature volume changes, unchanged immediate molecule inventory, conserved atoms, gaseous coefficients excluding solids/liquids, equal-gas exceptions, forward energy direction, initial equilibrium conditions for concentration edits, and rate/yield separation. Supplied later compositions are stationary examples, not claimed kinetic simulations.

## Questions after building

**Are there scientific mistakes?** All 36 comparison records, 20 numerical references and actual 3D geometry were checked. Two reserved numerical questions were revised to conserve the edited totals while retaining their starting equilibrium ratio at fixed temperature. Added starting-equilibrium conditions where necessary. Removed the false universal claim that counteraction can only be partial. Evidence explicitly supplies known dynamic equilibria: a flat trace alone does not establish that fact. Compression preserves atom sizes/bond lengths while changing occupied volume; rounded values are identified.

**Does the exam specification demand something different?** All AQA/Trilogy qualitative Higher changes are represented. The paired pressure question informed an individually authored appearance question using a supplied observation; the 3D scene does not invent an optical measurement from particle counts. Written explanations use rubrics and self-review, not automatically awarded examiner marks.

**Will this help students?** Thirty practice tasks have direct targeted recovery. Two reserved five-item forms use changed givens and whole-set feedback; two three-item reviews unlock after seven days. Wrong model predictions persist for correction. Model-assisted, hinted or repeated answers cannot become fresh independent evidence through an exposure alias.

**Is the UI comparable to Maths?** Each mechanism has its own representation and an explicit prediction/check operation. Actual lesson screenshots exposed default-looking controls and an empty failed-WebGL area; both were corrected. Narrow-screen checks now require readable labels, whole-word table headers, 44-pixel controls, keyboard operation and usable text alternatives. Actual desktop/mobile screenshots were inspected for all six activities, the 3D scene, independent questions, opening action and failed-WebGL fallback. The graph zero labels were separated after that review.
