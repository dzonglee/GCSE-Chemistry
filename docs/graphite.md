# Graphite: individual research and design

Twentieth lesson, individually reviewed. Research began after the diamond desktop/mobile screenshots and real GLB were inspected and sent. Graphene/fullerenes and polymers remain separate future work.

## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Inspect three coplanar bonded neighbours and hexagonal rings in actual stacked sheets. Predict what changes when one sheet slides: entire atoms and within-sheet covalent bonds move together, not individual bonds breaking. Predict the electrical carrier before advancing delocalised electron motion while carbon positions remain fixed. Compare with diamond, metals and small molecules without importing their incorrect explanations.

**Assets?** A real layered honeycomb network, rotatable/exportable, with no covalent sticks between layers. A labelled projection must show selected bonds and permit deliberate wrong predictions. Carbon positions, connectivity and electron inventory should be conserved under sliding/drift. Electron markers are schematic mobile carriers, not physical classical orbits or electrons attached to fixed owner atoms after delocalisation. Distinguish strong in-layer bonding from weaker interlayer attractions; finite edges omit continuation. An independent cropped representation must not give away its structure classification in a caption.

**Generic template?** Reuse the response/navigation shell and proven rotation/export mechanics. Diamond's four-direction connected network is the wrong scientific asset. A molecule viewer alone cannot teach sliding or carrier motion. Build graphite-specific honeycomb geometry, whole-layer displacement and electron movement, with wrong causal predictions retained.

## Actual sources read

Strong in-layer bonds and hexagonal rings are deliberate distractor features for these properties.
- Paired June 2022 Foundation 06.1 text and scheme: identify graphite from structural diagrams, correct C. The actual PDF page 22 diagram was inspected: extended hexagonal sheets contrast with a fullerene cage, nanotube and polymer; no interlayer covalent links are drawn.
- OpenStax m68770, publisher raw source at commit db0a8e6027100ce082e67fc8879faab86f9a58a7, HTTP 200 and actual graphite paragraphs/caption read. Strong bonding within sheets and weak attractions between sheets explain pencil material rubbing onto paper; graphite is an exception to a universal covalent-insulator rule. Advanced graphene extensions are reserved for the next separate lesson.

## Actual Maths and UI comparison

Read the Maths `SolidPartsAnswer` implementation: it projects real geometry, lets learners change view tilt, retains deliberate selections, supports independent responses and clears only chosen model work. This lesson likewise distinguishes geometry/view changes from answer predictions. It uses the established Maths-derived white-card, blue-action, visible-course-navigation layout, with native touch/key controls and the original first-useful-control viewport assertion.

The chemical asset has 96 carbons and 120 strong covalent edges in three separate 32-atom honeycomb sheets. Each sheet is connected, has nine complete six-membered cycles and has no covalent edge to another sheet. Selected sites have three equal-length, coplanar bond directions at 120°. The middle sheet is translated by one bond vector relative to the two outer sheets, with schematic spacing. This is original geometry; no stacking label or atomic spacing is required for GCSE memorisation. GLB export preserves actual transforms and connectivity rather than being a decorative screenshot.

## Mistakes found and corrected

Initial projections overlapped the sheets and obscured the learning target. The oblique fallback now separates the layers; the initial 3D angle reveals their separation while allowing rotation. Electron markers initially overlapped the enlarged selected-carbon highlight. The carrier view now uses equal-sized carbon markers and a separate mobile-electron pool; independent browser geometry verifies positive gaps between every core and electron marker. This does not imply electrons are owned by nearest atoms.

The recovery browser test initially used three as an intended wrong answer, which is actually correct for graphite; it now uses the four-neighbour diamond misconception and checks that this wrong draft remains unchanged after teaching and reload. A draft written-task schema mismatch was corrected to the existing rubric-array format before build validation. No assertions were disabled to make these checks pass.
