## Before authoring: design answers

**Interesting chemical thinking:** follow each O and carboxyl C through an ester junction; distinguish correct atom counts from correct connectivity; understand why both ends react and why a repeat boundary is not a molecular end.

**Interaction and assets:** the monomer-end editor separates the two O bonds of COOH and the OH hydrogens. Students edit one end at a time while both complete proposals stay inspectable. The repeat editor starts with no backbone atoms, lets students add/move/remove C, CH₂, O and H, select a separate oxygen bond order on C, and choose boundaries/brackets/count notation. Changes alter the actual diagram immediately. The existing scaffold builder remains useful preparatory practice.

**Difference from other lessons:** condensation consumes functional-group fragments and retains particular linking/carbonyl atoms. Addition changes C=C without a small by-product; its original content remains intact. A generic coefficient exercise, recognition bank or number line cannot test the missing connectivity decisions. New construction is bounded to supplied CH₂ spacers; it is not an unrestricted molecular drawing package.

**Why each addition helps:** the guided end task teaches C–O–H versus C(=O)–O–H and the second reactive end. The monomer practice retrieves both complete groups. The free-repeat practice transfers from supplied groups into a connected repeat. Two cold forms prevent copying an immediate reference; the short formula response matches an actual command. Two separate delayed forms retrieve the structures after a seven-day gap. Similar/equivalent responses share conservative exposure, so changed spacer numbers alone are not advertised as fresh learning.
