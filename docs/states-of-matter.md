## Individual implementation review

The lesson contains 48 original tasks: two warm-up, five refreshers, four guided tasks, twenty-one independent tasks, two five-question checks and two three-question delayed reviews. Two written explanations explicitly require self-review and never earn automatic correctness. The Higher-only sphere/force limitation task is labelled and excluded from common reserved forms.

**Specific interactions and assets:** actual equal-radius 3D particles can be rotated with pointer or keyboard and exported as binary GLB. The same 24 identities persist; solid frames vibrate about fixed sites, liquid frames change neighbouring identities among close disordered sites, and gas frames redistribute widely spaced positions. The accessible flat diagram is separately drawn rather than presented as an exact 3D projection. Four physical changes preserve all particles while requiring the correct energy direction and unchanged chemical identity. A proportional signed axis makes state prediction depend on supplied thresholds.

**Mistakes found and corrected:** the initial solid vibration allowed a sphere to protrude 0.009 model units through the floor. Moving the fixed sites inward corrected that geometry without relaxing the containment check. The journey-order expectation was updated to the actual prerequisite order: states precede ionic bonding. Browser assessment testing distinguishes the already exposed solid fact from the unexposed independent liquid diagram; different assessment items must not be given the same freshness expectation merely because they share a screen position.

**Actual Maths comparison:** NumberLineWorkbench uses proportional signed positions, native keyboard-accessible operations and retained history. This lesson applies those principles to a temperature axis and independently checked predictions, with undo/reset and saved incorrect choices. A generic particle selector would not teach fixed-position vibration, energy transfer, exact transition boundaries or molten-versus-aqueous symbols.

**Scientific scope:** particles may be atoms, molecules or ions depending on the material. Sphere positions, frame trajectories and radii are illustrative and do not calculate pressure, speed, density or force laws. Regular solid arrangement is identified as crystalline, not universal. State-change energy depends on the actual structure and attraction involved; the lesson does not claim all materials have weak intermolecular forces. Exactly at a pure-substance melting/boiling threshold at fixed pressure, both phases may coexist; no invented phase fraction is shown. Molten NaCl is (l), while (aq) means dissolved in water. Bulk properties are collective, not properties of one atom.
