# Group 7: halogens — individual design and source review

## Questions before authoring this lesson

**What does Maths do better than the preliminary route?** Read Maths's NumberLineWorkbench: visible position changes, meaningful bounds, native keyboard controls, history and interpretation of the current state. Apply that to temperature versus phase thresholds; use deliberate species/prediction choices for Chemistry. The old combined group route could not teach molecule/ion distinctions or test all displacement/no-displacement cases.

**What must this lesson let students manipulate?** A wrong single-atom/ion representation must remain visible until changed to a neutral two-atom molecule. A temperature control must change physical state while preserving intact diatomic molecules. A proposed displacement must be tested against reactivity and the actual dissolved halide, with a valid no-reaction outcome.

**What assets help?** A real rotatable Three.js particle asset with GLB export and 2D fallback, a qualitative phase diagram with six intact molecules, original before/after species cards and a partial neutral-atom shielding comparison. Use molecule count and charge explicitly, not decorative coloured circles without meanings.

**What distinctions are easy to get wrong?** Cl₂ versus Cl⁻; halogen versus halide; room state versus a supplied higher temperature; iodine's grey-black solid/purple vapour/brown aqueous solution; coloured added solution versus a displacement; chemical electron gain versus physical phase change; increasing boiling point versus decreasing chemical reactivity; ionic alkali-metal halides versus molecular hydrogen halides.

**How should it differ from Group 1?** Group 1 loses an outer electron more easily down-group; Group 7 gains an incoming electron less readily. Do not reuse the Group 1 direction or equation model. Preserve the group-specific variables and show the comparison's limits.

The full nine-case displacement matrix was checked independently: Cl displaces Br/I, Br displaces I, I displaces neither; same-element pairs have no net displacement. Iodine's brown aqueous appearance is not confused with purple vapour or organic-solvent appearance. The simplified ledger excludes concentration/equilibrium calculations.

Neutral-atom diagrams show only shell count and seven outer electrons; inner electron counts are omitted and no first20 arrangement rule is extended to Br/I. The solution's halide ions are distinguished from these explanatory neutral-atom comparisons. Greater nuclear charge is not ignored: the explanation states weaker overall attraction after distance/shielding effects. Formal Higher redox/half-equation grading is deferred rather than labelled Foundation-complete.

The original phase diagrams retain six diatomic pairs in every illustrated state. At rounded transition boundaries the science helper allows solid/liquid or liquid/gas coexistence. The native temperature control uses 10 °C steps, preserving a bounded saved history and signed-temperature interpretation. Molecular mass uses explicitly supplied AQA-rounded isotope averages, not single-isotope mass numbers.
