# Group 1: alkali metals — individual review

## Questions before the rebuild

**Is it up to Maths's teaching standard?** The old route compressed Groups 1, 7 and 0 into four questions with a generic prediction model. Its interactions did not distinguish observations, products, causal trends or conservation. The new Group 1 journey follows Maths's visible construction/mistake-repair pattern, native controls, model history, faded support and reserved assessment. It is not a claim that the whole course matches Maths.

**What specifically does this lesson need?** First-three reactions with water/oxygen/chlorine, observable evidence distinct from inference, one outer electron and +1 ions, increasing reactivity despite increased nuclear charge, trend-based predictions, physical properties and balanced water products. Halogen displacement and noble-gas trends require their own individual rebuilds.

**Which interactions/assets help?** Compare a chosen metal/reactant: product names and reported observations change while the nucleus and outer-electron principles remain explicit. A lithium-reference/comparison shell view connects extra inner shells to shielding and distance. A coefficient workbench preserves wrong atom counts until repaired; changing coefficients cannot change substance formulae. The lithium-ion task uses the existing actual rotatable 3D atom scene. These distinct assets serve different reasoning demands.

**How should the UI behave?** Metal/reactant native controls appear at the top; observation and interpretation have separate visible labels. Two shell diagrams compare evidence, stacked at mobile width. The equation has four labelled controls, an explicit expression and left/right ledger. Answer inputs are separate from model controls. Practice removes the model initially.

**What misconceptions need repair?** More protons alone determining reactivity; all Group 1 atoms having the same shells; losing protons to form ions; naming a gas from bubbles alone; hydroxide/chloride/oxide confusion; changing subscripts to balance; equating trend estimation to exact measurement; universal ignition or universal simple oxide products.

NaOH/LiOH are ionic formula units, not labelled molecules.

First-three typical water observations are bounded: lithium generally does not melt; sodium commonly melts/moves; potassium often ignites but ignition is not guaranteed. No numeric bubbling rate is invented. First-three float behaviour is not extended to every heavier alkali metal.
