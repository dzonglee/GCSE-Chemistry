## Independent calculations and scientific decisions

| Case                            | Independently recomputed result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main equal 35/37 population     | `35 × 50 = 1750`; `37 × 50 = 1850`; `(1750 + 1850) ÷ 100 = 36`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Main 75/25 population           | `35 × 75 = 2625`; `37 × 25 = 925`; `2625 + 925 = 3550`; `3550 ÷ 100 = 35.5`.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Every live slider position      | For mass-35 percentage `a`, mean is `37 − 0.02a`. All 21 permitted positions from 0 to 100 in steps of 5 are checked by independent per-atom summation. At 0 the mean is 37; at 100 it is 35; at 50 it is 36. “Strictly between” is used only when both populations are present. |
| Count-table difficult case      | `6 + 4 = 10`; `30 × 6 = 180`; `32 × 4 = 128`; `180 + 128 = 308`; `308 ÷ 10 = 30.8`. Distances from 30 and 32 are 0.8 and 1.2. Dividing by 100 gives the incorrect 3.08.                                                                                                          |
| Carbon starting construction    | `p=6, n=6, e=0`; `Z=6`, `A=6+6=12`, charge `+6−0=+6`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Carbon after six electrons      | `p=6, n=6, e=6`; `A=12`, charge `+6−6=0`; nucleus unchanged.                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Aluminium starting construction | `p=13, n=13, e=13`; `A=26`, charge `+13−13=0`. It is not labelled aluminium-27 yet.                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Aluminium after one neutron     | `n=27−13=14`; `14−13=1` added neutron; `p=13, n=14, e=13`; `A=27`, charge 0.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

Mass numbers approximate relative isotope masses in these supplied classroom examples; no exact natural abundance is asserted. Relative atomic mass is dimensionless, abundance counts atoms rather than sample-mass fractions, and a population mean does not imply fractional neutrons. Electrons have a small mass but do not enter mass number. Adding neutrons changes mass number without charge; changing protons changes element identity. Existing source-review documents retain the AQA/Pearson/OpenStax references; those external source documents were not freshly re-researched in this implementation turn.
