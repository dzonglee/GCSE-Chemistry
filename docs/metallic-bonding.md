## Before building

**Interesting/interactive?** Distinguish the particles that hold a metal together from those that move through a solid to carry charge. Then compare attempted layer displacement in a pure metal and a distorted alloy, without treating harder as higher melting or entirely non-conducting.

**Assets?** A positive-core/delocalised-electron diagram with a conserved electron/core ledger, selected charge-carrier arrows, explicit electrostatic-attraction explanation and separate pure/alloy layer comparison. A native proposed-carrier selection should remain wrong until repaired. Layer shift must demonstrate continued bonding, not disappearance of every attraction. Diagram positions, electron paths and relative atom sizes are illustrative; no numerical conductivity or hardness is inferred from decorative geometry.

**Generic template?** The navigation/answer shell is useful. The original ionic melting slider cannot explain metallic attraction, solid electron conduction or alloy layer distortion. Use distinct causal models; cross-structure evidence comparisons remain useful independent transfer work. A 2D projection must explicitly retain the giant three-dimensional structure rather than imply only a sheet of atoms.

Do not claim every metal is high-melting.
- Pearson 1.40–1.42: malleability, conduction, representation limitations and qualified “most metals” properties.
- Actual paired AQA 2023 Higher 06.2: describe metal conduction; scheme separately rewards delocalised electrons, carrying electrical charge and moving through the metal. 06.3 supplies lower alloy conductivity in the wire context; scheme links different-sized atoms/distorted structure to restricted electron movement. It does not say alloys have no electrons or never conduct.
- Actual paired AQA 2023 Foundation 04.1–04.2: six lead atoms plus twenty-four tin atoms requires denominator thirty, yielding twenty percent; distorted layers are caused by different sizes, not different shapes, and cannot slide easily. Percentages of atoms are not automatically mass percentages. The solder example is an assessment/data source, not laboratory guidance.
- Actual publisher [OpenStax solid structures m68770](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-chemistry-bundle/blob/db0a8e6027100ce082e67fc8879faab86f9a58a7/modules/m68770/index.cnxml), reobtained online HTTP 200 (34,177 bytes), relevant paragraphs read: metallic solid/malleability/conduction, wide melting-point variation including mercury/alkali exceptions, physical-evidence classification. The simplified electron-sea description is a model; do not depict positive cores as bare nuclei or use one universal metal ion charge.
