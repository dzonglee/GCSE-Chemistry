## Scientific review

Plants respire in both daylight/darkness; photosynthesis needs light, so net exchange depends on both rates. Microorganisms digest organic matter and use some carbon in aerobic respiration, releasing CO₂; some carbon enters microbial biomass. Soil mineral ions have separate root uptake/use, not supplying all glucose carbon. Aerobic respiration is specified because anaerobic products depend on the pathway. Feeding, death and waste have partial fates; not all dead material instantly becomes fossil fuel. Suitable burial preserves some ancient organic material over geological time; coal chiefly plant-derived, much oil/gas marine organic-derived. Carbonates can form sediments/limestone. A stated acid reaction releases carbonate CO₂ without a universal claim about every natural weathering process. Oceans exchange in both directions; net uptake is not permanent one-way trapping. Carbon atoms are conserved while compounds and store sizes change. Energy flows and dissipates rather than cycling identically to carbon. Budget masses are carbon alone, not whole CO₂, and refer to fixed boundaries/equal intervals. Deforestation can release biomass stock and reduce uptake; regrowth has limits, timescales and later-release possibilities. Seasonal dips need not cancel a longer rise; compare like seasons separately from different-season endpoints. Arbitrary concentration units are not ppm. A curve does not prove every cause or an exact future climate outcome. Old generic respiration wording was narrowed to aerobic respiration.
