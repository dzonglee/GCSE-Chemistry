## Before building: design questions

**Is this interesting and interactive?** Learners balance whole ion groups and see both signed charges and resulting atom counts. A neutral but non-simplest aluminium/sulfate ratio remains visible as a distinct error. Formula writing then requires independent capitals, subscripts and parentheses rather than selecting only a prepared answer.

**Which assets are useful?** Native whole-ion count controls, charged group tokens, a signed ledger, a readable atom-count table and a learner-written proposed formula. This lesson's demand is symbolic grouping, so these representations are more useful than another 3D lattice. Every token denotes a whole ion, not a molecule or scaled physical structure.

**What requires a distinct lesson?** The preceding transfer lesson does not teach nitrate/carbonate/sulfate/hydroxide formulas, repeated group parentheses or naming limits. A generic “cross the charges” template risks changing internal atom counts and accepting unsimplified ratios. Given positive and negative ion formulas must remain fixed throughout manipulation.

The ledger checks +2/−2, +6/−6 and atom totals independently. Ca(NO₃)₂ has Ca₁N₂O₆; Mg(OH)₂ has Mg₁O₂H₂; Al₂(SO₄)₃ has Al₂S₃O₁₂; (NH₄)₂SO₄ has N₂H₈S₁O₄. Numbers inside ions never change during balance. A neutral 4:6 aluminium/sulfate ratio fails the simplest-ratio criterion; a 3:2 ratio is non-neutral (+5).

## After building: review questions

**Mistakes corrected?** Syntax validation caught unquoted parenthesised formula keys during authoring. Scientific marking review avoids rejecting unnecessary parentheses around a single unchanged group, while preserving required repeated-group brackets and chemical capitals. A missing-bracket MgOH₂ answer remains visible and incorrect rather than being automatically repaired. Visual inspection found an unstyled compact atom table; full-width styled rows now support comparison. Final checks and screenshots are recorded below.

**Exam-board differences?** Common Foundation/combined formula reasoning, with Pearson's explicit given-polyatomic scope. Ammonium and specified Fe³⁺ are clearly supplied-ion extensions, not a claim that all such ions must be predicted from groups or memorised. Endings are bounded by actual ion names; not every oxygen-containing compound is -ate. Practical salt preparation, covalent group structure, broader organic naming and complete OCR mapping remain separate work.

**Student usefulness?** The simultaneous charge and atom ledgers explain why a numerically neutral ratio can still be wrong as a simplest formula, and why a missing parenthesis changes composition. Independent typed formulas require symbolic construction, not recognition alone. The written response requires reasons rather than a crossing shortcut. This is design judgment, not student-study evidence; written marking remains self-review.

**Maths UI comparison?** Read the actual Maths RatioWorkbench: conserved total, one-part controls, explicit parts, proposed quantity and feedback. The chemical model borrows those explicit unit/total principles while keeping ions indivisible and their internal formulas fixed. Actual desktop/mobile Maths ratio screenshots are captured for comparison. Formula controls have 44-pixel targets, undo/reset/reload, compact charged tokens and an atom table. Final desktop/mobile Maths ratio and Chemistry hydroxide/charge-ledger views were inspected. The atom table now fills the model width with aligned, separated rows; the first useful ion operation remains within the original tested mobile viewport.
