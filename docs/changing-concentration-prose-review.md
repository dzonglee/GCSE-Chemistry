## Teaching and numerical review

Checked dilution against removing a well-mixed portion: dilution keeps solute mass while increasing final solution volume; removing a homogeneous portion reduces both mass and volume proportionally, leaving concentration unchanged. For example, 10 g in 500 cm³ diluted to 1000 cm³ gives 10 g/dm³, whereas retaining 250 cm³ leaves 5 g and still gives 20 g/dm³. The retained and removed portions account for the original solute.

Checked the other numerical examples individually, including final volume versus solvent added, mass divided by volume in dm³, and combined mass/volume factors. Evaporation examples require the specified nonvolatile solute and absence of precipitation or other loss. The existing 3D markers represent grams, not individual molecules.

Reviewed the relevant source sections of the Maths inverse-proportion and ratio-concentration-conversion lessons. The fixed-numerator denominator reasoning is useful here; mixture-part ratios must not be presented as mass concentration.
