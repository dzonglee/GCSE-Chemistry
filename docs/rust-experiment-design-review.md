## Confirmed demand and individual design

AQA 8462 4.10.3.1 explicitly requires students to describe experiments and interpret results showing that both air and water are necessary for iron rusting. The original Materials123-task state included genuine live oxygen/water/outcome predictions, controlled six-result comparisons and written coating interpretation. It did not directly require students to describe how to establish and maintain the contrasting experimental conditions. This correction addresses that specific response-demand gap without replacing those activities.

Ten individually authored tasks add three mechanism/error recovery questions attached to the existing native rust-condition model, one guided full plan, two written independent practice descriptions/criticisms, a two-item independent form and a two-item delayed form. The full response specifies wet air, genuinely dry air and genuinely oxygen-excluded water, comparable clean iron nails, duration/temperature/exposed area, repeated rust observations and the two correctly paired conclusions. It explains removing a reactant versus preventing its return. An oil layer over ordinary water does not remove pre-existing dissolved oxygen; a stopper alone does not make water oxygen-free. Different nail composition or initial rust confounds a comparison. Two no-rust controls alone do not establish rust in the both-reactants condition.

School-supervised preparation examples use a suitable drying agent/closed dry-air tube and dissolved-gas removal/prevention of re-entry in the water-only comparison. Scientifically valid alternative exclusion arrangements remain eligible for manual review. The app presents simulated planning and supplies no home practical workflow. The existing live condition proposal is relevant chemical manipulation; no decorative replacement apparatus is introduced. Written plans keep raw multiline work and receive concrete examples/criteria, not automatic keyword examiner marks or grade predictions. References remain sealed until whole-set submission and delayed retrieval requires seven real elapsed days.

## Exact corrosion clause evidence

The actual4.10.3.1 statement was reread in full. The lesson concept defines corrosion as destruction by chemical reactions with environmental substances, distinguishes iron rusting and teaches all three named barriers: grease, paint and electroplating. Native r-paint/r-scratchPaint cases and written p-scratch/cB-barrier require the barrier mechanism and its failure at a scratch. The named grease/electroplating examples are taught in the concept; they do not each have a dedicated delayed written recall item. Protective aluminium oxide is explicitly taught and assessed in p-aluminium and vA-oxide. Native remaining-zinc/scratched-silver/zinc-gone comparisons, p-scratch and manual cA-zinc require sacrificial protection through relative reactivity, remaining contact and consumption. Controlled p-rust uses all six results and requires reactant comparisons, coating interpretation and a limit on conclusions. The new ten tasks close the explicit experiment-description gap. These distinctions record where facts are taught, practised, interpreted and manually reviewed rather than pretending every named example has a separate written retrieval question.
