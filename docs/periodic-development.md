# How the periodic table developed: individual review

## Design questions considered for this lesson

**Maths standard?** Read the neighbouring Maths FactorListWorkbench: deliberate construction, visible intermediate state, feedback, history and keyboard controls. This lesson uses those principles for evidence-based classification rather than another atom-count sandbox. Its current two-arrangement puzzle is narrower than an open construction workbench; it supplies a focused comparison, not a claim of complete product parity.

**Interesting interaction?** Students compare an incorrect, fully filled weight-ordered arrangement with a repaired arrangement containing a gap. E's observed behaviour stays unchanged; only its proposed position changes. They predict the missing family member, then independently judge matching and conflicting discoveries.

**Assets and 3D?** Original two-dimensional property cards are appropriate here: the reasoning concerns columns, missing members and evidence. The existing atom lessons provide genuine 3D assets where spatial inspection helps. A spatial periodic-table decoration would not strengthen this classification task.

**Distinct lesson needs?** Historical information available at the time must remain separate from modern explanation. Mendeleev did not arrange his first table using known proton counts or electron shells, and argon was discovered later. The isotope-weighted average explains mass-order exceptions retrospectively.

**Layout?** One native arrangement control before the card grid, model feedback adjacent to it, then the separate answer. Property text remains visible with the chosen arrangement. Three compact columns retain the family comparison on mobile without page overflow. Independent practice removes the workbench.

**What must students explain?** Missing discoveries, inappropriate chemical grouping under strict weight ordering, gaps, changes of order, predicted properties, supporting/conflicting discoveries, and isotope abundance versus proton number. Names are tied to contributions rather than an unrelated date quiz.

Written self-review behaviour remains unchanged. First mobile opening control ended about 6 pixels below the viewport. Shortened the prompt/instruction, preserving the unchanged viewport assertion. Visual review also shortened a narrow mobile gap label to avoid breaking a word, and screenshot capture returns the written response to its opening line. Wrong placement remains visibly wrong until repaired; checked feedback distinguishes the property conflict. Reload, undo/reset and strict saved-model validation preserve the deliberate comparison.

**Content limitations?** The comparison is a simplified two-row, three-family puzzle, not a full reconstructed historical table. Written explanations are self-reviewed and never automatically correct. The specification comparison is bounded to the sources listed; full course mapping, OCR, full papers, practical coverage and demonstrated learner exam readiness remain outstanding.
