# The periodic table — individual lesson review

## Before implementation

**Maths standard?** The old route combined a particle-count sandbox and six brief periodic/history questions. It did not ask students to make a position prediction or interpret unfamiliar-element chemistry. The new journey follows the inspected Maths construction, mistake repair, faded support, independent transfer and retrieval structure.

**Interesting and interactive?** Students repair an incorrect proposed sodium placement, then place oxygen from its arrangement. Group and period choices move the proposed symbol without silently correcting it. Magnesium links Group 2 to losing two electrons while keeping its nucleus, with the actual existing 3D asset showing the resulting ion.

**Assets?** An original proposed-position grid, supplied shell diagrams, complete first-20 reference, and real 3D magnesium ion. The reference is an auxiliary resource; the student's placement and electron-loss operations are the learning models. The partial grid uses GCSE group labels and explicitly omits transition/later period-4 elements.

**Distinct needs?** Atomic/proton order, row/column vocabulary, outer electrons versus occupied shells, helium's full two-electron shell, unfamiliar main-group transfer, chemical and physical classification, general metal/non-metal regions, hydrogen, graphite and boundary ambiguity. Historical table development and detailed reactivity trends have separate lesson demands.

**Layout appropriate?** Two compact native placement controls before the grid, model checking near them, independently labelled answer fields, and collapsed full reference. Practice removes the workbench. Mobile labels must align and the first useful control must remain in the opening viewport.

## Actual materials read

- [AQA Chemistry specification](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/chemistry/specifications/AQA-8462-SP-2016.PDF), 4.1.2.1–4.1.2.3, plus the bounded graphite/delocalised-electron statement in 4.2.3.2. Historical 4.1.2.2 was read to identify the separate next lesson rather than imply it was covered here.
- [Pearson Chemistry specification](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/GCSE/Science/2016/Specification/gcse-chemistry-spec.pdf), March 2024, 1.13–1.20: distinguishes table development, atomic-number order, position/classification and electron arrangements. Historical scope remains separate.
- [AQA June 2023 Higher paper](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-84621H-QP-JUN23.PDF) and [scheme](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-84621H-MS-JUN23.PDF), 01.3 and 08.1–08.3: unfamiliar-element outer count, full-shell explanation, same-group compound prediction and boundary classification. The 08.3 scheme accepts several differently justified predictions for tellurium, including an inconclusive prediction. The app therefore does not enforce an absolute left/right rule near the boundary.
- [AQA June 2023 periodic-table insert](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes/2023/june/AQA-8462-PT-JUN23.PDF), identified from the official catalogue, downloaded as a verified PDF, text read and actual single-page table visually inspected. It uses Groups 1–7/0; the key distinguishes relative atomic mass from atomic/proton number. The app's first-20 reference gives proton number and arrangement, avoiding presentation of a selected isotope mass number as relative atomic mass.
- [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, The Periodic Table](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-chemistry-bundle/blob/db0a8e6027100ce082e67fc8879faab86f9a58a7/modules/m68695/index.cnxml): actual publisher paragraphs on modern order, periods/groups, characteristic metal/non-metal properties, intermediate metalloids, main-group behaviour and hydrogen's distinct properties. Its modern Groups 1–18 are translated explicitly; its modern Group 7 passage concerns transition elements and is not imported as GCSE halogen terminology.

The original tables, questions and meshes do not reproduce official artwork or questions. The source paper's compound-formula prediction also depends on covalent bonding; this lesson's elementary ion prediction does not claim to complete that later bonding demand.

## After implementation

**Mistakes?** Proposed wrong placements remain wrong until students change the controls. Group and period have separate feedback. All first-20 positions were checked against an independent reference, including helium and the fourth period. Ion operations preserve the magnesium nucleus. The first-20 reference has all twenty entries and correct group/arrangement relationships. Singular/plural feedback and wrapped mobile labels were corrected during visual review.

**Exam differences/gaps?** This is bounded introductory position/classification teaching. Group-specific reactivity trends, detailed covalent formula prediction, transition-metal properties and historical periodic development remain their own lessons. Main-group first-20 rules are not extended to transition-metal configurations. Boundary predictions need evidence rather than forced classifications. Written explanations are self-reviewed and no official extended/partial-mark scoring is claimed.

**Useful to students?** Wrong positions, outer/shell confusion, isotope mass confusion and simplistic property rules become visible and recoverable. Unfamiliar-element practice demands transfer beyond reciting the first twenty. General rules and exceptions are both taught. These are design reasons; effectiveness has not been measured with learners.

Types, lint, formatting, build and 47 unit checks passed. Full browser baseline: 140 passed, 0 failed. After the added region-pattern task, singular/plural corrections and mobile-label alignment, all 12 lesson-specific desktop/mobile checks passed. Actual placement, magnesium 3D ion and boundary-evidence samples were inspected and sent before proceeding. The original atom-count reference test was replaced by a substantive complete first-20 reference check because this lesson now uses placement rather than the old count sandbox; all twenty rows, sodium/magnesium arrangements, helium Group 0 and accessibility remain checked.
