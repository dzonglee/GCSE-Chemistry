## Design and review questions answered
What must students reason about? Product stages before/after the shop; equivalent service, consistent boundaries and transport; summing like quantities; fixed versus repeated reuse impacts; equality versus strictly lower whole-use count; conflicting energy/water/waste; measurable inventory versus effect allocation/value judgements; advertising limitations; finite-resource reduction and actual collection/sorting/recovery.

Which interactions fit each demand? Six dedicated activities/15 fixed cases: classify four events in each of three lifecycles; construct complete or selective stage boundaries in two original datasets; build two numerical stage inventories including a signed difference; compare10/20/30 reuse counts with different outcomes; construct two three-measure trade-offs without an invented environmental score; build three material-recycling balances. Original source graphs use one common energy axis and remain fixed under wrong calculations. Boundary subtotals are expressly learner-selected, not automatically certified full-life values. Optional source graphs are available in independent questions as supplied evidence; live proposal support is removed.

Why this visual form? Boundary cards, comparable bar graphs and separated usable/non-product material streams expose the actual reasoning. Rotatable objects would not establish environmental boundaries or effects. Graph labels must remain legible at320/390px; the first useful keyboard-controlled stage selector must fit the unchanged664px opening requirement. Controls, Undo/Clear/reload and wrong raw numeric strings must work on touch and keyboard.

Which misconceptions are addressed? Only manufacture matters; omit inconvenient stages/transport; add the same journey twice; compare different functions; reusable always wins; count fixed energy per use; silently change washing rules; equality is strictly lower; add litres andMJ; lower energy implies every impact lower; renewable means zero impacts; every assessment disagreement is dishonest; technical recyclability guarantees local recovery; collection mass equals usable material; recovery applies to unsorted mass; rejected atoms disappear; every recycled material needs the same purity; resource reuse creates unlimited stock.
