## Before-design questions

What should students do? Move metals into a justified order; distinguish conditions and observations; predict deposited metals; reason from complete, partial or conflicting evidence; identify controls and distinguish timed progress from final yield. These require five different native activities, rather than a navigation-only widget.

What deserves 3D? One representative displacement event. The actual asset conserves the same seven atomic constituents between two states: one added metal surface atom, one dissolved metal cation, and intact SO4²⁻. Aqueous ions remain separate rather than becoming fictitious sulfate salt molecules. Zn, Mg or Fe can displace Cu; reverse and same-metal examples retain the original states. Sulfate's four oxygen positions form a real tetrahedron with dot products −1/3 and equal 0.48 model-unit connections. Connections express geometry, not Lewis bond order. Water, hydration and the full metal lattice are explicitly omitted.

What does Maths do? Its actual ordering-integers and inequalities-on-number-lines source and four live desktop/mobile guided views were reviewed. The Chemistry ordering activity uses visible tiles and keyboard/touch move buttons. Reactivity position is ordinal; equal gaps are not equal numerical differences. Missing comparisons remain unknown.

## After-design review

Products and equations distinguish room-temperature water/hydroxide from suitable dilute HCl/chloride. Brief absent magnesium bubbles do not prove no possible reaction; copper/HCl does not generalise to oxidising acids. Carbon and hydrogen are labelled nonmetal references. Greater positive-ion tendency supports the series without claiming the Higher half-equation lesson is covered.

Comparisons explicitly state conditions and observable completion. Two comparisons above a third metal cannot order the top pair. Conflicting displacement directions under identical suitable conditions require investigation. Unmatched mass or exposed area can defeat a comparison; equal final hydrogen volumes without times do not establish equal rate or reactivity.
